It's the first half of the 19th century. France has just had its July
Revolution, once again replacing a king with an emperor, or a king with a king,
or, well, someone with someone or something else. Steam technology is
proliferating, factories are replacing workshops, railways are spreading across
the world. The USA is buying slaves by the shipload, a pandemic is sweeping
across Europe, and Karl Marx is a teenager. People are watching wondrous,
seemingly godlike technologies become commonplace in the space between their
adolescence and adulthood. Fear of another revolution has each State escalating
its violence to new levels.

200 weavers have gathered in a city in France and are on their way to a new
automated weaving factory owned by some rich guy. The rich guy hears about this
and sends word to the local military, who send word to the local police. Two
cavalry units and a cityfull of police rush to the factory to prevent who knows
what, but it's too late: the weavers have destroyed basically every piece of
automated weaving equipment in the place, and are now running pell-mell through
the alleys. The State manages to capture about 30 of these people, and punishes
them harshly.

These days we call these people Luddites, a synonym for "people who hate
technology."

I love technology. I've been an engineer this last decade, founded a software
engineering co-op with one purpose being to expand the ability of other
engineers. I run local LLMs on my computer and manage a homelab running dozens
of services. I'm also a Luddite.

The Luddites didn't hate technology, they hated these new changes being wrought.
They didn't like that the technology they thought would decrease the
strenuousness of their labor was instead causing them to be replaced by
automated machines and losing all their income. They didn't like that their
position as craftspeople who determine the quality of clothing and how it's
distributed was being replaced by factory owners churning huge volumes of cheap,
quick-to-degrade cloth out that was, despite being lower quality, driving down
the cost of all cloth globally. They hated this new invention that was moving
power away from workers and into the hands of the rich: Capitalism.

How has technology changed our world? It's given us global warming, factory
suicides, [Instagram doomscrolling](/posts/psychic-attack.md), Tik Tok
misinformation, tech surveillance, and
[unemployment](/posts/ai-will-take-your-job/). Is this an inherent function of
this technology, though? Did we have to continue to pursue fossil fuels after
inventing renewable energies, to fuel cars that are more inefficient than the
earlier invented streetcar and train? Did we need to make factory conditions so
terrible that people would rather jump off the roof than spend another day
sticking together iPhones? Did we need to fill our photo sharing apps with
addictive hate-mongering content designed to make us upset, and ads designed to
make us buy things we don't want, or feel bad about ourselves if we don't buy?
Did we have to lock life saving medicine behind monopoly?

The negative externalities of technology aren't inherent to technology itself,
but instead inherent to the capitalist mode of production. Like the Luddites, we
don't hate technology, we hate Capitalism.

Well, that's fine then, we can still have Capitalism, so long as we regulate it,
right? We can use the power of liberal democracy, or even democratic socialism,
or perhaps even communism (with "Chinese characteristics") to regulate these
evil capitalists, to make sure they don't take things too far!

How's that working out for us? Microsoft was convicted of being a monopolist
decades ago, and still runs a dominant position in the operating system space,
driving the majority of offices around the world. Trump ordered Microsoft to
shut down the email account of an ICC prosecutor that was annoying him, and
[Microsoft complied](https://www.euractiv.com/news/microsofts-email-shutdown-of-icc-prosecutor-fuels-eu-fears-of-us-tech-blackmail/).
The European Union forced Apple to finally use a usbc port instead of whatever
proprietary nonsense they were using before, but then turned around and allowed
[mass surveillance](https://edri.org/our-work/chat-control-what-is-actually-going-on/)
of its citizenry. In the PRC, workers replace factory suicide with languishing
under a 996 work schedule - but boy do they get great State-ensured benefits!
Just enough bread, just enough circus; delivery drivers and camgirls circling
the drain. Whatever the liberals, social democrats, and state capitalists
(sorry, "Chinese Communists") are trying, isn't working.

It doesn't have to be this way. People have been resisting, turning these tools
back against the capitalists that would exploit us, or the States that would
repress or harm us.

The liberals, the demsocs, and the state capitalists have failed to prevent
capitalists using technology to exploit us (or directly aided them to do so), so
how can we emulate those above to leverage technology to fight back and liberate
ourselves? We already have some of the tools, and we have the means to build new
ones. We should, or really must, do so, because technology is a force
multiplier, and a force multiplier is needed to counter the massive resources
working against us. Also, if we want to create a newer, better, world, we have
to have the means to bring along the comforts people in this world cling to. In
order to avoid getting split like the Soviets by blue jeans, we need to ensure
people have iPhones under Communism.

When I [gave a talk about this](https://calebjay.com/talks/ntusocialists/), a
question that came up was whether these sorts of tools are only being used by
super-geeks, and that leftists don't have as much integration into these
communities; or, that these tools are perhaps too hard to use; or that the
alternatives, such as Instagram, are just too attractive to leave. How do we
break free, and help others break free?

I'm addicted to social media, but I'm
[sober now for several years](/posts/how-to-quit-social-media). Something that
helped me break free was realizing that every time I post content on reddit or
twitter, I'm basically giving money to Elon Musk or some other blood drinking
techno-feudalist pedophile. I'm increasing the value of the platform, and
providing training data for their AI. Makes using the sites feel dirty.

At the same time, usage of these platforms is normal; they spend millions
staffing psychiatrists, who are [tweaking psychic knobs](/posts/psychic-attack/)
to keep us addicted to their platforms. We hardly have the tools to resist this,
yet.

It's a question though: how can we get more people on these platforms? For one
thing, FOSS software is often buggy, or ugly, so we could certainly use more
eyes on this software, engineer or otherwise. FOSS is often
resource-constrained, so it's an area where it's very easy to see the impact of
an individual, which can be quite rewarding. Tired of participating in protests
with thousands of people and not seeing any change from it? Try picking a FOSS
project (maybe one of the above!) and participate in its community, offering
design suggestions or making bug reports. It's quite the feeling to see your own
contribution on an app that thousands or even millions of people use.

However it doesn't fully answer the question. I don't know how to get people to
stop doing things that hurt themselves. I don't even know how to get ME to stop
doing things that hurt me! I leave that question to smarter leftists than me:
how do we get people off the tools that are hurting us? How do we get more
people to voluntarily participate in their own liberation? What kind of tools
are we missing to fight back, liberate people, or create parallel forms of
resource distribution and mutual aid?

If you're interested in reading about leftist techno-utopian musing, I suggest
"Walkaway" by Cory Doctorow and "A Half Built Garden" by Ruthanna Emrys.
Generally [Cory Doctorow's blog](https://pluralistic.net/) is a good source of
info for this sort of thing as well.
