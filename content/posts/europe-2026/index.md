Oddly Tammy and I find ourselves yet again going to Copenhagen for her business trip, then to Paris for a friend's wedding. Two in just half a year, lucky us! Even better, last time I was working, and so had to spend most of my time in the (beautiful) Copenhagen library or cafes on my laptop, but this time I was able to fit more actual tourism into my schedule. Although I did end up spending a lot of time at the library anyway working on [508](https://508.dev), Mandarin, or random FOSS stuff.

This year was supposed to be my year to get back into writing, and only now I seem to have time. I have a theory I'm working on that a place can only be really experienced either in person or through the mind of another person, and the mechanism to communicate the experience from the mind of the in-person individual to the not-in-person individual must be one that activates the imagination of the latter, else said person will have a shallow impression of the location. That shallow impression will then represent for them the location wholly, whenever mentioned or thought of.

So for example, I could show you a picture of how beautiful the "suburbs" of Freetown Christiania were, but the pictures, no matter how well taken, would still be shallow compared to my own experience of it, and therefore your impression of Freetown Christiania will also be shallow. Instead I want to try to transmit as much of the experience holistically into your mind through the best way we've figured out so far: written word. I'm on the fence whether that's better than spoken word, maybe not? Well, you're here, so let's try.

Last quick thought: this ties into another theory I have that everything between the printing press and hypertext was a mistake (radio, film, television), and then so too was everything after hypertext (social media photo sharing, short form videos like tik tok or reels). I think there's something special about writing, and I guess all book nerds probably think that, but after reading [How to Take Smart Notes](https://blog.calebjay.com/posts/books-2025/#how-to-take-smart-notes-one-simple-technique-to-boost-writing-learning-and-thinking-s%C3%B6nke-ahrens) I'm very convinced writing is a special form of thinking.

## Day 1: Malmo, Sweden

I landed early and did my usual thing of dodging plane staff trying to force me to wait for my betters to take their time packing their things and getting off the plane. Good thing too, apparently the line for immigration at CPH can take an hour or more, but since I was one of the first to arrive, I only had to wait a couple minutes. Also the dude behind the counter had the same watch as me, nice. 

Stood in front of a board of trains at 6am, trying to figure out the rejsekort app Tammy sent me, gave up when my credit card failed to register. Bought a ticket for the wrong destination, realized five minutes before getting on the right train, and in a blind haste somehow got the rejsekort app working. Double ticketed ride to Sweden. Plan was to spend the weekend in Malmo to hang with our friends and their kids there. I'm somehow wired, maybe delirious as I entered hour 26 of a fast (why waste calories on plane food?), so I get recommended this TODO almond croissant shop and hop off the train early to go. To my Taiwan-roasted body, it was gorgeously cool; jacket weather, but I went without to just let the cold get into my muscles, a feeling I very rarely get to experience now. 

Croissant was mind blowing, every part of it baked to caramel, filled with chocolate because there are no gods. Swedes apparently don't say "bye bye" like we do in Taiwan so I make myself look like a moron by saying it twice thinking they didn't hear me the first time on my way out the door. Multiple people looked at me, nobody said bye bye back, fantastic!

Hotel lets me check in early. It's in an old part of town, cobblestones, great to walk on when I've got 10kg on the back. They have a bench press in their basement. Somehow I have enough go-juice to bang out a morning lift, quick shower, and then head to our friends' apartment. 

Walk through layers of town: old old town, new old town, old new town. The entire walk smells like cardamnon and cinnamon. It's strawberry season; I buy some from a lady that points at her hijab like I'm stupid when I ask if she's Swedish - how should I know! Then she said she'd live there since she was a kid. So you are Swedish then! Great strawberries though.

Their apartment is on the top floor, large, airy, sunny; you can see the ocean from their livingroom. Five minute walk from the preschool the older kid goes to. Loooong parental leave. No wonder I saw hordes of kids in strollers on the walk over. On that note, Scandanavians just leave their kids in their strollers outside while parents go in to shop, even in the rain, so as we go into a cafe for fika we walk past pairs of little elvish kids getting rained on, hah hah punks. Tammy arrives from her workday in Copenhagen.

Trying a rule on this trip to help maintain my girlish figure: as many pastries as I want, but only one bite each. After the almond croissant I changed it to two bite; one to taste it, another one to double check. So we get the very Swedish cardamom buns and "Chocolate ball," latter of which is somehow umami chocolate and nearly makes me break my two bite rule (I found it in my bag later that night and finished it so in the end it did win). Kids seem to turn the smallest thing into a day trip, so we ended our short day with falafel in the store my friend's 3 year old says has a "dark cave" (a kitchen). Big pile of not too greasy meat and very moist falafel, haven't had good versions of either since my last trip to Europe, so it's a nice way to finally get some protein.

Finish the night getting hit in the face with a sledgehammer of jetlag and falling asleep above the sheets.

## Day 2: Lundt, Sweden

A full day lets us do a day trip, the with-children equivalent of a three book Mordor saga. Weather misbehaves so ohhh nooo we have to stop in a cafe for pastries and coffee. I get a big blob of coconut and some kind of "danish" with a pile of caramlized almonds on top, easily the best thing out of our little group buy, I completely flavormogged Tammy and our friends. Swedes are tall and yet I still smack my head while standing up to go to the bathroom, and again entering the bathroom, and again exiting the bathroom. Their buildings are so old, which I like, except when their ceilings assault me. Cafe probably predates the settling of the town I grew up in in the States. 

We wander to the old Cathedral, which absolutely predates the settling of the town I grew up in, as well as the entire USA itself, and probably Columbus. Bizarrely, the entire outside of the Cathedral is covered with scorch marks, from "the fire." Which fire? The. When? Unknown. How does stone burn? Also unknown. I'll look it up one day. Drizzly and a bit chilly. Inside is paper-dry, warm and full of candles fulfilling some religious purpose - reminds me of incense in a Taiwanese temple. They have this great big "astrological" clock just sort of set against one of the walls, each wheel with spider-thin annotations of time, month, season, who knows what else. There's a crypt, with massive slabs of carved stone set into the floor, people carved on them. Bones underneath? Unknown, very cool. Centerpiece is a massive tomb, comes up to my shoulder, *definitely* has bones. Bits of gold accents hammered into the stone here and there, possibly explains the bars on the tiny little windows where the wall and ceiling of the crypt meet. 

After, a secondhand shop, where I, incredibly, find pants AND a shirt that fit. A validation of all my hard work in weight loss this year. Excitement as well since I clearly underpacked for the weather, and now I'll be warm when I want to be. That's about all the daytrip we can manage with the kids.

Afternoon is spent preparing for a little hodge podge dinner in the apartment. We grab some meatballs and a slice of cheesecake from TODO, and deliver it alongside some bread and cheese I found at this TODO little shop in Lundt. Finally, good bread and cheese, nearly impossible to get in Taiwan. We have a nice little picnic in the apartment. The meatballs are moist and the sauce is rich and creamy, I end up constantly dunking my bread in it. Cheesecake is absurdly decadent, thick crumbly caramel crust. Barely manage to keep it to two bites.

Another early night lets us squeeze in a bit more hangout with the kiddos the next morning.
