Oddly Tammy and I find ourselves yet again going to Copenhagen for her business trip, then to Paris for a friend's wedding. Two in just half a year, lucky us! Even better, last time I was working, and so had to spend most of my time in the (beautiful) Copenhagen library or cafes on my laptop, but this time I was able to fit more actual tourism into my schedule. Although I did end up spending a lot of time at the library anyway working on [508](https://508.dev), Mandarin, or random FOSS stuff.

This year was supposed to be my year to get back into writing, and only now I seem to have time. I have a theory I'm working on that a place can only be really experienced either in person or through the mind of another person, and the mechanism to communicate the experience from the mind of the in-person individual to the not-in-person individual must be one that activates the imagination of the latter, else said person will have a shallow impression of the location. That shallow impression will then represent for them the location wholly, whenever mentioned or thought of.

So for example, I could show you a picture of how beautiful the "suburbs" of Freetown Christiania were, but the pictures, no matter how well taken, would still be shallow compared to my own experience of it, and therefore your impression of Freetown Christiania will also be shallow. Instead I want to try to transmit as much of the experience holistically into your mind through the best way we've figured out so far: written word. I'm on the fence whether that's better than spoken word, maybe not? Well, you're here, so let's try.

Last quick thought: this ties into another theory I have that everything between the printing press and hypertext was a mistake (radio, film, television), and then so too was everything after hypertext (social media photo sharing, short form videos like tik tok or reels). I think there's something special about writing, and I guess all book nerds probably think that, but after reading [How to Take Smart Notes](https://blog.calebjay.com/posts/books-2025/#how-to-take-smart-notes-one-simple-technique-to-boost-writing-learning-and-thinking-s%C3%B6nke-ahrens) I'm very convinced writing is a special form of thinking.

## Flight In

Nothing that interesting, but while walking around Abu Dhabi airport, I was thinking about how "Western" fashion seems to have completely taken over the world. Civilization 4 terms: American culture victory. Communism defeated by the need of the people for blue jeans. When trying on wedding clothes in Taiwan, it's all western style suits (bizarrely including [zoot suits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoot_suit)). However, in Abu Dhabi, I saw all sorts of people in all sorts of clothes I never really see, like heavy white cloth wrapped every which way around people, or various kinds of robes. I liked it. Keep it up, that region of the world!
